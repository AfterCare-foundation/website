# The Symbolon

**Raw material — not homepage copy.** Held for a future about page, founder
story, or press backgrounder. Surfaced by Dr. Halvard Ing (wildcard
paleographer) during the copy panel that produced the current hero.

---

## The short version

AfterCare's torn card is not a new idea. It is the oldest
privacy-preserving authentication device in the Western record, and it has
been independently reinvented at least three times.

**σύμβολον (symbolon) — ancient Greece.** Two guest-friends who might not
meet again for a generation would break a token — a knucklebone, a potsherd,
a clay tablet — and each keep a half. On a later meeting, or between their
descendants, the halves were fitted together. If they matched, the bond was
proven. No registry, no witness, no names written down. The word is the
literal root of English *symbol*: from *sym-* (together) + *ballein* (to
throw) — the thing thrown together.

**The indenture — medieval Europe, c. 12th century onward.** A contract was
written out twice on a single sheet, then cut apart along a deliberately
irregular, tooth-like line. Each party kept one half. Neither half proved
anything on its own; the two together proved everything, because only they
matched. The name comes from that cut — *indented*, toothed. English
apprenticeship contracts carried the name for eight hundred years.

**The tally stick — English Exchequer, c. 1100–1826.** A debt was notched
across a hazel rod, then split lengthwise. The creditor kept the longer
piece (the *stock* — hence *stockholder*), the debtor the *foil*. The
grain of the wood made each split unforgeable, and the two halves
authenticated each other on sight.

## Why it matters for AfterCare

The pattern in all three: **the security model is the object.** Nothing is
recorded anywhere. No third party holds a list. Identity is never asserted —
only *correspondence* is, and only by the two halves meeting again. That is
exactly AfterCare's threat model, arrived at from the opposite direction.

Three lines this could support, if a page ever needs them:

- The torn edge is not decoration. It is the whole security model, and it
  is nine hundred years old.
- We didn't invent this. We just stopped asking for a name back.
- Two halves that only match each other, and prove nothing apart — that is
  a symbolon, and it is where the word *symbol* comes from.

## Why it is deliberately not on the homepage

**Ursula Vane (panel):** a hero has room for one idea, and the reader
holding an actual card needs the everyday version, not the etymology. Step 1
now says *"like a cloakroom stub"* — a concrete object the reader has
already held, doing the mental-model work in four words.

**Dr. Ing concurred:** *"The mythology is for the second conversation, once
someone already cares. Lead with it and it reads as a project more
interested in itself than in the person holding the card."*

Use this material where a reader has already opted in to a longer read — an
about page, a grant application's narrative section, a press backgrounder,
a conference talk. Not the hero.
